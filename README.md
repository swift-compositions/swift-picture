# swift-picture

Use this package when you describe a frame as a value and hand it to something that draws it: flat shapes with fills and strokes, gradients and images, text, groups under a transform and a clip, layers with opacity, blend modes and effects, additive particles, and meshes seen through a camera. Start from `Picture`, a list of `Picture.Element`s. Set a string with `Picture.Text`, which turns a font file and a string into glyph outlines at places, so that text reaches a renderer as paths. Draw through `Picture.Renderer`, a value holding one closure that draws a picture into a `Raster<Compositing.Premultiplied<Float>>`; it is a `Renderer.Protocol` of swift-renderer and a `Witness.Key` of swift-witnesses, so a renderer is installed and replaced per scope. The `Picture` product fixes what a picture means and gives the exact arithmetic a renderer needs (a colour's pixel, a paint's pixel at a point, the point an effect reads, a sprite's light, where a scene puts a point); the `Picture CPU` product draws with it: `Picture.Renderer.cpu` is a renderer in pure Swift for every platform, the reference for any faster one, and the live, preview and test value of the witness. The `Picture PNG Integration` product turns a drawn raster into a `W3C_PNG.Image`; it is empty unless the package's `PNG` trait is on, which is what brings swift-w3c-png into the graph. If you only need pixels, use swift-raster; if you only need paths, use swift-geometry.

## Laws

- Laws that begin "A renderer" bind whoever draws a picture; the tests of `Picture CPU` check them for `Picture.Renderer.cpu` as far as it draws: shapes, fills, strokes, text, groups, clips and layers without effects. A layer with effects, sprites and scenes fail with `.unsupported` until it draws them, so those laws are not yet tested.
- Picture space is the raster's own. A point (x, y) of `Picture.Space` is the position (x, y) of the raster a picture is drawn into, in pixels, as swift-raster has it: the origin is the outer corner of the first pixel of the first row, x grows along a row, y grows from row to row, and pixel (x, y) covers the square from (x, y) up to (x + 1, y + 1). Rows are shown top first, so on a screen y points down. swift-geometry names things for y up, so its "counterclockwise" and "lower-left" look clockwise and upper-left on screen; winding rules and stroke outlines do not depend on that. Geometry is `Picture.Plane`, which is swift-geometry's `Geometry<Double, Picture.Space>`, so a path is a `Picture.Plane.Path` and a point a `Picture.Plane.Point<2>`; pixels are `Float`.
- A picture never depends on the raster's size: drawn into a larger raster it shows the same things at the same pixels, and what falls outside is not drawn.
- `Picture.Color` is a colour as written: sRGB-encoded red, green and blue with straight alpha. Nothing is clamped, and two colours are equal when they are written the same. `linear` puts each of red, green and blue through `IEC_61966.SRGB.TransferFunction.linear(_:)` and leaves alpha alone. `pixel` is that multiplied by alpha, a `Compositing.Premultiplied<Float>`. This is the only way a written colour becomes a pixel, so everything a picture mixes, it mixes in linear light.
- A `Picture` is its elements in order, the first at the back. `appending(_:)` lists this picture's elements and then the other's, and `Picture.empty` is neutral on both sides. Pictures are equal when their elements are equal in order. Every part of a picture is `Sendable` and `Hashable`.
- A `Picture.Shape` is a path with a fill, a stroke or both. A renderer draws the fill first and the stroke on top. The fill covers the points the path contains under the fill's `Winding.Rule`. The stroke covers the points its outline contains under `nonzero`, and `Picture.Shape.Stroke.outline(of:tolerance:)` is that outline: the path's `stroked(_:tolerance:)` of swift-geometry with the stroke's pen, failing with the same `Geometry.Path.Error`. A renderer puts the paint's pixel at each covered point over what is behind, with the share of a pixel's square that is covered as coverage.
- `Picture.Paint.pixel(at:)` is the pixel a paint gives at a point of the shape's space. `solid` gives its colour's pixel everywhere.
- `Picture.Gradient.pixel(at:)` reads the stops in the order given. Before the first stop's offset, and for a parameter that is not a number, it is the first stop's pixel; from the last stop's offset on it is the last stop's pixel; between two stops it is the first one's pixel interpolated to the second's by `Compositing.Premultiplied.interpolated(to:at:)`, so colour and alpha move together, premultiplied, in linear light. An offset below the one before it counts as equal to it. Two stops at one offset are a hard edge that takes the later stop at that offset. No stops give transparent.
- `Picture.Paint.Linear.parameter(at:)` is how far along the line from `start` to `end` the point's foot lies, 0 at `start` and 1 at `end`, and beyond both; a line with no length gives 0 everywhere. `Picture.Paint.Radial.parameter(at:)` is the distance from the centre in radii; a radius that is not positive gives 0 everywhere. The paint's pixel is the gradient's pixel at that parameter, so a gradient holds its end colours outside 0 to 1.
- A `Picture.Image` holds a raster of working pixels. With the identity `placement` its first pixel's corner is at the origin and one pixel is one unit. `placement` moves it as a group's transform moves its content: `pixel(at:)` samples the raster at the point the inverse placement gives, with the image's own sampling and edge, mixing by `interpolated(to:at:)`. By default sampling is bilinear and outside the raster is transparent. A placement that cannot be inverted squashes the image to no area and it paints nothing; so does a point that is not finite. A raster with no pixels paints its edge's constant, or nothing.
- A `Picture.Group` holds a picture, a transform and an optional clip. A renderer draws the content's point p at `transform.applied(to: p)`, so a group inside a group draws at `outer.multiplied(by: inner)`. The clip is in the content's space and moves with it. A renderer draws a group's elements straight onto what is behind, each limited by the clip: where the clip covers a share c of a pixel, the pixel ends as what was there plus c times the change the element would have made. Clips inside clips multiply. A group with the identity and no clip draws exactly as its elements would in its place.
- A `Picture.Layer` holds a picture, effects, an opacity, a blend mode and an operator. A renderer draws the content on a transparent sheet of its own, applies the effects in order, and then puts each pixel of the sheet onto the pixel behind it with `Picture.Layer.composited(_:over:)`, which is `composited(over:operator:blend:coverage:)` of the W3C compositing target with the opacity as coverage. By default there are no effects, the opacity is 1, the blend mode is `normal` and the operator is `sourceOver`. Shapes inside a layer see only the sheet, never what is behind the layer.
- A `Picture.Effect` reads the sheet as a picture on the whole plane, transparent where nothing was drawn, in the units of the layer's own space; a renderer must not let the edge of its raster show in the result. Every effect but `blur` names the points it reads, and a renderer reads the sheet there.
- `blur` is a convolution with a Gaussian of the given standard deviation along both axes; a deviation that is not positive changes nothing. `Picture.Effect.Blur.followed(by:)` is two blurs as one: the deviation is the root of the sum of the squares.
- `zoom` is a radial blur: a renderer gives each point the mean of the sheet at `source(of:at:)` for fractions from 0 to 1. That point runs along the line toward the centre, from the point itself at fraction 0 to `amount` of the way to the centre at fraction 1. The centre reads itself, and an amount of 0 changes nothing.
- `chromatic` moves red, green and blue apart: a renderer gives each point `pixel(red:green:blue:)` of the sheet's pixels at the point minus the red offset, minus the green offset and minus the blue offset. That pixel takes each channel from its own pixel and the largest of the three alphas, so three equal pixels give that pixel and no channel exceeds the alpha.
- `pixelation` fills every cell of a grid with what is at its centre: `source(of:)` is the centre of the cell holding the point, where a cell holds its lower edges and not its upper ones and one cell's corner is at `origin`. It reads its own result unchanged. A cell with no width or height reads the point itself.
- `kaleidoscope` cuts the plane around `center` into `segments` equal sectors, the first starting at the direction `rotation`, each sector being two mirror-image wedges. `source(of:)` is the point's mirror image in the first wedge, at the same distance from the centre, so a point in the first wedge reads itself and the result reads itself. With no segments, and at the centre, a point reads itself. Angles come from the scalar's own `atan2`, `sin` and `cos`, so the result is as repeatable across platforms as those are.
- `displacement` reads each point from `source(of:)`, the point plus `offset(at:)`. The offset's x is `amplitude` times `Noise<3, Double>` of swift-noise with the seed, at (x × `frequency`, y × `frequency`, `phase`); its y is the same with the seed's second word one higher. Each is at most `amplitude` in size, zero where all three noise coordinates are whole numbers, and bit-identical on every platform, as that noise is.
- `Picture.glow(deviation:strength:)` and `Picture.backdrop(_:clip:)` add no element kinds; they are compositions. A glow is the picture followed by a layer of the same picture, blurred, with `strength` as opacity and `lighter` as operator. A backdrop pane is the picture followed by a group with the clip, holding a layer of the same picture under the effects with `copy` as operator: inside the clip the picture is replaced by itself blurred or bent.
- `Picture.Sprites` are discs of light. `Picture.Sprites.Profile.coverage(at:)` is the share of a sprite's light at a distance from its centre given in radii: `hard` is 1 inside the radius, `soft` is (1 − d²)², and both are 0 from the radius on. `light(at:)` is the sum over the sprites of each one's pixel times its coverage there. It is a sum, so the order of the sprites does not matter up to rounding, and it can exceed 1. A renderer adds that light to what is behind with `lighter`. A sprite with no radius gives no light.
- A `Picture.Scene` draws meshes through a camera into a rectangle of the picture. `Picture.Scene.Camera.clip(_:in:)` is projection times view times model, applied to (x, y, z, 1), with `Matrix(projection)` of swift-matrix. `normalized(_:in:)` divides by the fourth clip coordinate and is nil unless that is positive, so a point in the plane of the eye or behind it has no place. `Picture.Scene.position(of:in:)` maps normalised x from −1…1 onto the viewport's x range and normalised y from 1…−1 onto its y range, so up in eye space is toward smaller y, the top of the screen; its depth is the normalised z, lower for nearer points.
- `Picture.Scene.Camera.direction(_:in:)` is a direction of a model in eye space at unit length: view times model applied to (x, y, z, 0), so no translation moves it. It is the zero vector when that has no length. It is right for normals as long as view and model do not scale unevenly.
- A renderer draws a scene straight onto what is behind, model after model. Where two things of one scene fall on the same point, the one with the lower depth shows; depth is not compared across scenes. What lies in front of the near plane or beyond the far plane is not drawn, and a face or edge that crosses the near plane is cut there. Both sides of a face are drawn. `flat` fills every face with its colour. `wireframe` draws every distinct edge once, as a line `width` picture units wide whatever its distance. `points` draws every vertex once, as a disc of `radius` picture units whatever its distance.
- `glass` is drawn after every other model of its scene and shows only its nearest surface. Where that surface is not hidden, the pixel becomes `Picture.Scene.Material.Glass.pixel(over:)` of the picture as it stood before any glass, read at the point plus `offset(for:)` of the surface's unit normal in eye space there. The offset is `bend` times the normal's x, and minus `bend` times its y, so a surface facing the eye reads straight through. The pixel is the tint over what was read; the default tint is clear and changes nothing.
- `Picture.Text` sets a string in a font read by swift-iso-14496-22. Every code point but U+000A becomes exactly one glyph, in the order of the string, through the font's character map; a code point the font does not map becomes glyph 0. There is no shaping: no ligatures, no combining, no reordering, no fallback font, and left to right only. The one adjustment between glyphs is pair kerning.
- The pen starts each line at x 0 and moves by each glyph's advance width plus `kerning(left:right:)` with the glyph after it on the same line, all times `size` over the font's units per em. A glyph's `origin` is where the pen stood, on the line's baseline; its `advance` is its own advance width at that size, without kerning.
- A glyph's `outline` is the font's outline scaled by `size` over units per em around the glyph's own origin, with the font's up turned toward smaller y, so it stands upright on a screen. `path` is the outline moved to the glyph's origin. `Picture.Text.path` is every glyph's path in order, and `shape(_:)` is that path with a fill. A renderer never sees a font.
- U+000A ends a line, also an empty one, and kerning does not reach across it. Baselines are `leading` apart, the first at y 0. `ascent`, `descent` and the default `leading` are the font's `ascender`, minus its `descender`, and their sum plus `lineGap`, at the text's size; a leading given wins.
- With a `measure`, a line takes as many words as fit: it breaks before a word that follows a U+0020 when the line up to the end of that word would be wider than the measure. The spaces at the break stay at the end of the line as glyphs and do not count toward its `width`. A word wider than the measure stands alone on its line and is not broken. Without a measure a line ends only at U+000A.
- Lines are aligned in the measure, or without one in the width of the widest line: `left` starts a line at 0, `center` at half of what is left over, `right` at all of it.
- `Picture.Text.Error` is `size` or `measure` for one that is not positive and finite, `units` for a font whose em has no units, `font` with the font package's parsing error for bytes that are not a font or kerning data that is damaged, and `outline` with its outline error for a glyph that cannot be read.
- `Picture.Renderer` is a `@Witness` struct with one closure, `render`, which takes the picture borrowed and the raster `inout` and fails with `Picture.Renderer.Error`. The macro's labelled method `render(picture:raster:)` and the `render(_:into:)` that makes it a `Renderer.Protocol` of swift-renderer stand side by side and both call the closure once.
- `Picture.Renderer` is its own `Witness.Key`, and the conformance lives in `Picture CPU`, the one target that sees a renderer: its live value is `Picture.Renderer.cpu`, and preview and test follow live. Importing `Picture` alone gives the type, not the key. `Picture.Renderer.absent` draws nothing and fails with `.absent`. `Picture.Renderer.Error` is `Hashable`. A renderer set with `withWitnesses` is the one `Witness.Context` gives inside that scope, and only there. `unimplemented()` fails with `.unimplemented`, carrying the witness's own error.
- A renderer draws onto the raster it is given, over what is already there, and changes nothing when it fails before drawing. The same renderer on the same machine gives equal rasters for equal pictures.
- `W3C_PNG.Image.init(_:)` of `Picture PNG Integration` makes an RGBA image of 8 bits a channel from a raster of working pixels, marked `sRGB` with the perceptual intent and the sRGB gamma, the raster's rows top first. Each pixel's colour is divided by its alpha, held to 0…1, put through `IEC_61966.SRGB.TransferFunction.encoded(_:)`, multiplied by 255 and rounded to the nearest whole number, halves up. Alpha is held to 0…1, multiplied by 255 and rounded the same way, without the transfer function. What is not a number counts as 0, and a pixel with no alpha is transparent black.
- Each of the 256 sRGB levels written as an opaque `Picture.Color` comes back as that level, and so does each of the 255 alphas above zero written with white. A raster with no pixels fails with `W3C_PNG.Image.Error.dimensions`, as PNG has no empty image.
- `Picture.Renderer.cpu` of `Picture CPU` draws on one thread into a copy of the raster's pixels and puts the copy in place only when the whole picture is drawn, so a failure anywhere, however deep in a layer, leaves the raster exactly as it was. Nothing it does depends on time, threads or addresses, so the same picture on the same machine gives the same raster bit for bit; bit-equal rasters across machines are not promised.
- It covers a path with `Raster.coverage(of:rule:in:tolerance:)` of swift-raster, the exact area of the path flattened to within 0.1 pixel of the raster, over the pixels its bounding box reaches. A stroke's outline is made in the shape's own space, so a group that scales scales the pen, at 0.1 pixel divided by the root of the sum of the squares of the transform's linear part, and filled under `nonzero`. A paint is read once per covered pixel, at the pixel's centre taken back into the shape's space. A transform that cannot be inverted draws nothing, though a stroke the geometry refuses still fails.
- A layer's sheet covers only the pixels its composite reads, cut to the box outside which its content draws nothing. An operator that changes the backdrop where the sheet is transparent (`clear`, `copy`, `sourceIn`, `destinationIn`, `sourceOut`, `destinationAtop`) changes every pixel of the canvas inside the clip; the other seven leave such pixels exactly as they are and are visited only where the sheet holds something.
